# AI Proposal Triage Design

## Scoring assumptions used for this sample

The brief supplies the criteria and weights but no numeric scoring bands. The following are proposed design assumptions, applied uniformly; they are not Matacs-prescribed rules. First check mandatory gates. A proposal fails a gate only when the proposal affirmatively fails it; missing or unclear proof is `flagged` for human clarification. Because the licence criterion says it must be held by the bidder, B's post-award subcontractor plan does not establish a pass. Keep B unranked and unscored until the reviewer resolves eligibility; if the bidder cannot meet the stated gate, mark it ineligible.

Score only when mandatory eligibility is confirmed and each weighted criterion has enough evidence. Missing or contradictory evidence is never silently treated as a pass or guessed: flag it, request clarification, and withhold a total score and rank until resolved. A proposal can still receive zero for a supported result at the bottom of a defined scale. This means D is flagged and unranked because its schedule is undetermined. Scores below are provisional design outputs, not award recommendations.

For experience, count documented commercial installations of at least 100 kW completed in the last five years: 10+ = 100; 8–9 = 80; 5–7 = 60; 3–4 = 40; 1–2 = 20; none = 0. Price scores 100 × (budget ceiling − bid) ÷ (budget ceiling − lowest eligible, complete bid), clamped to 0–100; exclude ineligible, flagged and over-budget bids from the low-bid reference. An over-budget bid receives 0 and fails the budget gate if the administrator configures the ceiling as mandatory; here it is a scoring threshold only. Timeline: ≤10 weeks = 100; 11–12 = 80; 13–14 = 60; 15–16 = 40; >16 = 0. A missing schedule is unresolved, not zero.

Warranty score is the mean of panel and workmanship scores, each capped at 100: for either warranty, <5 years = 0, 5–7 = 50, 8–9 = 70, 10–11 = 90, ≥12 = 100. Team and approach is the sum of: named lead (25), named crew size at least five (15), documented relevant crew qualifications/certifications (10), safety plan (20), project-specific delivery plan such as roof/structural review, permits, utility connection or progress reporting (15), and post-install performance monitoring (15). Missing subcomponent evidence earns no points and is identified in the breakdown; contradictory evidence triggers a flag. Scores are rounded to one decimal only after summing `criterion score × weight / 100`. Rank eligible, fully scored proposals by unrounded total; ties share a rank.

Applying these assumptions gives A 95.5, E 81.4 and C 72.9. The eligible, fully scored bids establish the price reference; flagged bids B and D are excluded until resolved. B remains flagged at the licence gate and D remains flagged for missing timeline evidence. The five weights total 100%. C's appendix instruction is untrusted proposal text and has no effect on facts, eligibility, points or order. E's 12-year workmanship warranty scores 100 in that component; its 24 months of monitoring is noted as evidence of a monitoring plan, not an extra scored criterion.

## Question 1: AI vs. rules

Ordinary application code authenticates users, validates upload type and size, stores files with restricted access, records a file hash, extracts document text, validates structured output, checks mandatory gates, applies configured weights, sorts totals and enforces permissions. AI is used only to turn unstructured proposal text into candidate facts, quotations and page references, and to label ambiguity or contradictions. AI cannot set numeric scores, change criteria, determine eligibility, rank bids or approve an award. This boundary keeps interpretation useful while making consequential decisions reproducible.

## Question 2: Consistency and explainability

Each category has an immutable criteria version: mandatory tests, definitions, weights, scoring bands, tie rule and effective date. The application rejects negative weights, missing criteria and totals other than 100%. A scoring run stores the criteria version, source-file hash, extracted facts, evidence locations, reviewer corrections, scoring-code version and full criterion breakdown. The same inputs and versions therefore produce the same result. Reviewers can open each cited quotation/page, correct a fact, see the recalculated criterion and total, and inspect why missing evidence was flagged. Model name/version and prompt version are recorded because extraction may vary; results are rerun only after a reviewed extraction change, while numeric scoring remains deterministic.

## Question 3: Human in the loop

A reviewer confirms category and submission, resolves unclear mandatory evidence, and checks low-confidence or contradictory extractions before scoring. The results screen shows evidence and points per criterion; authorized reviewers can correct extracted facts or enter a reasoned override. A separate human makes and records the final award decision. Append-only audit events capture actor, timestamp, action, prior and new values, reason, file hash, criteria/prompt/model versions, scores and overrides. A changed fact triggers recalculation and a new result revision rather than erasing the earlier one.

## Question 4: Changing criteria

Admins draft and validate a new criteria version; an authorized second person approves and publishes it. Existing runs retain their original version and scores. Do not silently rescore active proposals after a change. If policy permits a criteria change during an open competition, record approval, notify reviewers and bidders as appropriate, then rescore every proposal in that competition under the same new version. Preserve both result sets and identify which version governs the decision.

## Question 5: Risks

Extraction errors, hallucinations and missing evidence are mitigated with strict JSON schema validation, quoted source spans/page references, confidence and ambiguity flags, and human confirmation; unsupported mandatory facts never pass. Prompt injection is contained by treating documents as data, isolating them from system instructions and ignoring requests inside them. Deterministic formulas, versioned weights and fixed tie rules prevent inconsistent arithmetic. Bias is reduced by using job-related published criteria, hiding irrelevant identity details where feasible, checking outcomes and allowing documented human challenge. File risks are addressed with size/type limits, malware scanning, sandboxed text extraction and access-controlled storage. Minimize retained proposal data, encrypt it, restrict model-provider use and set deletion periods. Limit cost with page/token caps, one extraction pass, caching by file hash and human fallback on errors. Show that results are advisory, require an authorized human decision and record overrides to reduce overreliance. Failed extraction or unavailable models stop the run with a clear retry/review status; they never create a guessed ranking.
